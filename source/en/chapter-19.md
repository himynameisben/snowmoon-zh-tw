At that point, they heard a faint buzz that began to rapidly get louder. Almost immediately, a few small drones, similar to those that would check Zei for cheating equipment in the Minpentai championships, flew into view.

"Yep! Pretty standard in Veridia. We really care about having a clean environment. We breathe twelve kilograms of air every day, more than what we eat and drink combined. So we're really all under-rating how important the air is."

"The average concentration of carbon dioxide in our atmosphere is 300 parts per million. Air that we breathe out is 40,000 parts per million. So if the air you're breathing has 700 parts per million, then either there's some other source bringing that extra CO2 in, or, more likely, one percent of the air you're breathing in is air that someone else breathed out. Gladias found a book about Dzego architecture, titled "zui fia pai dan". He flipped through it. It contained pictures of many designs of basement rooms, shelters and underground rooms inside of mountain pyramids. The book showed them in roughly chronological order starting shortly after the war against the Arctic Empire a hundred years ago.

The earlier designs were very practical in construction, with not much thought put to comfort, or even safety against non-human threats. The designs in the middle started resembling ordinary rooms, with the main glaring exception being lack of windows. And finally, the designs near the end of the book were very well-decorated and comfortable, even more so than above-ground construction. About half had imitation windows, pretending to be outside. The other half did not have windows, and instead had plants, well-decorated walls, and other objects that seemed carefully chosen to make the inhabitant not see any space that they might think is supposed to have a window.

"Gladias!", Zei shouted. "Come back, it's almost done!"

Gladias turned around, and walked back. Just as he arrived, the last progress bar reached 100%. Another window automatically opened. A script began running. "Reconstructing data... estimated time remaining 175", it said onscreen - not in Dzegoban.

Gladias and Zei sipped their tea, and stared at the timer counting down. The librarian, curious, hovered quietly about two meters behind them.

"Okay, so this is a language model. But how large was that file? After the data reconstruction."

"10.7 gigabytes. The overhead was exactly a factor of six. I had a background thread check where the files were from, they were all recently uploaded. One sixth was a few recent movies - public data, though of course *the selection of which movies they chose* is a secret key of sorts. Another half was a big pile of someone's health data. The last third was also video, though it seemed to be AI-generated. It was all Arctic sermons talking about how fundamentally flawed the Veridian and Dzego civilizations are."

"The recombination algorithm was a five-of-six erasure code of five pieces of 10.7 gigabytes each. Each of those pieces, in turn, was a steganographic encoding: they used language models to find bits to flip so that the data still looked plausible, while encoding the actual underlying message, at a very ambitious rate of one bit of plaintext per four bits of ciphertext. And then the movies were a sort of cryptographic pad: if you don't know the exact set of movies that were used, even if you could fully uncover the plaintext, it would just look like random data."

"Wait, if they wanted a pad, couldn't they just pseudorandomly generate one by repeatedly hashing some data?"

"Ah actually you're right. Maybe I was overthinking it, maybe they just did it this way to throw people off."

"Okay, so 10.7 gigabytes, what is it?"

"It's not a plaintext language model. It's an obfuscated language model. Level two obfuscation, much weaker parameters than the full cryptographic-grade parameters that are used in the cryptographic networks for voting in DU or Bansunpei or for that matter the Order of Steering, but it's still the same algorithm."

"Level two obfuscation is actually the right tradeoff for language models, because language models themselves are big and compute-intensive, so they can't handle the high overhead of going level four, and on the other hand, language models are at least partially obfuscated and resistant to bit flips all on their own. If you uncover the value of a few weights, or get the ability to reliably flip a few values in a few tensors, it's not like you can get anything useful out of that."

"Overhead factor of level two obfuscation is in the tens to low hundreds on size, and high hundreds to low thousands on compute. So this model is pretty tiny; I suspect whoever sent this fine-tuned it to only do this one task. I also think this language model's wrapper program, the full package that's being obfuscated, is doing the thinking all at once, to verify whether or not my answer is satisfactory. If it is, it will just give us its message at the end."
